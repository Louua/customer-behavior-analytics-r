# Customer Behavior Analytics & Segmentation Using R

End-to-end exploratory data analysis, customer-level **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary)** modeling, and **K-Means clustering** on the **UCI Online Retail** transactional dataset.

## Business Problem

A UK-based online retailer holds over 540,000 historical transaction records without a structured view of revenue seasonality, product/country concentration, order distributions, or behavioral customer segments to guide retention and marketing decisions.

## Analytical Questions

1. How does revenue change over time?
2. Which products and countries contribute most to sales?
3. How are order sizes and customer spending distributed?
4. Which customers purchase recently, frequently, or spend the most?
5. Can customers be grouped into interpretable segments via RFM + K-Means?
6. What are the limitations and evidence-based business actions?

## Dataset & Setup

- **Source:** [UCI Machine Learning Repository — Online Retail](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/352/online+retail)
- **Raw file:** `data/raw/Online Retail.xlsx` (`541,909` rows × `8` columns, `2010-12-01` to `2011-12-09`)
- Download the archive from UCI and extract `Online Retail.xlsx` into `data/raw/`.

### Exploratory Data Analysis
- **Total valid gross sales:** `£10,642,111` across `19,960` invoices (`£533.17` mean vs. `£303.30` median invoice value), `4,338` identified customers, and `3,922` distinct products.
- **Seasonality:** Monthly revenue rises sharply in Q4 from `£1,056,435` (Sep 2011) to a peak of `£1,503,867` across `2,769` invoices (Nov 2011); Dec 2011 (`£637,790`) is partial (ending Dec 9).
- **Market concentration:** United Kingdom accounts for `84.6%` (`£9,001,744`) of revenue. Netherlands (`£285,446`, `9` customers) and EIRE (`£283,141`, `3` customers) reflect concentrated wholesale accounts, whereas Germany (`94` customers) and France (`87` customers) have broader bases.
- **Product anomalies:** Non-merchandise fee codes (`DOT` Dotcom Postage, `POST` Postage, `M` Manual) and a single `80,995`-unit invoice (`23843`) appear in top-10 gross revenue rankings alongside broad-demand bestsellers (`22423`, `85123A`, `85099B`).

### Customer Segmentation (`K = 4`, Log-Scaled RFM, Silhouette = `0.335`)
Reference date: `2011-12-10` (`4,338` customers).

| Segment | Customers | Share | Median Recency (days) | Median Frequency | Median Monetary (£) | Total Revenue (£) | Revenue Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **High-Value Loyalists** (Cluster 3) | 728 | 16.8% | 9 | 10 | 3,688 | 5,788,710 | 65.1% |
| **Mid-Tier Regulars** (Cluster 1) | 1,163 | 26.8% | 54 | 4 | 1,342 | 2,095,526 | 23.6% |
| **Recent Low-Frequency** (Cluster 4) | 876 | 20.2% | 19 | 2 | 440 | 451,892 | 5.1% |
| **Inactive / One-Time** (Cluster 2) | 1,571 | 36.2% | 184 | 1 | 299 | 551,081 | 6.2% |

- **Statistical checks:** Spearman rank correlation between `frequency` and `monetary` is `0.807` (`recency` vs. `frequency`: `-0.563`; `recency` vs. `monetary`: `-0.482`). Kruskal-Wallis test confirms significant spend separation across clusters (`chi-squared = 3078.3`, `df = 3`, `p < 2.2e-16`).
